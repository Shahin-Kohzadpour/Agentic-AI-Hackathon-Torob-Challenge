The previous prototype could fabricate shopping evidence, lose decisions on refresh, and stop after a seller failure. This change delivers a Persian shopping workflow backed by the complete Torob catalogue, persistent sessions, strict purchase constraints, six clearly labelled seller simulations, recovery to a real alternative offer, and an evidence-based visit card.

The primary interface is a touch phone experience: compact editable purchase conditions, a visible latest assistant reply, a single-row composer, 44 px primary touch targets, keyboard-aware positioning, product comparison, map/list views, actual Neshan routing, image model extraction with user confirmation, and sourced shopping-area suggestions. Larger screens retain the phone canvas. Deployment uses Liara with a 4 GB instance and persistent storage; credentials and dataset files remain outside Git.

Validation: 57 service tests including all 40 acceptance scenarios; TypeScript, ESLint, production builds and GitHub CI pass. Touch-enabled Chromium exercised the complete seller-failure journey, reload persistence, two-user isolation, purchase-condition changes, widths 320/360/390/430 and a short 480 px viewport. Live image/model/routing, rejected forged actions and controlled unavailable-voice/map-tile fallbacks were also tested. Actual Liara redeploy, rollback, restore, restart and consistent backup preserved session evidence. Versioned evidence is recorded in the delivery report.

Limitations: seller contact is simulated, no stock is reserved, dataset shop names/phones are anonymized, and speech accuracy depends on browser support and has not been measured. No customer reviews or real-time Torob prices are invented.
